# Style Guide

This document captures the intended visual and brand direction for Brian Cusuanto's personal website.

## Brand goals

The website should feel:

- credible
- modern
- sharp
- technical
- approachable
- professional without feeling corporate-generic

## Overall direction

- The site should feel more intentional than a standard portfolio template.
- The homepage should feel like a modern personal/business landing page.
- The portfolio page can remain more evidence-heavy and technical.
- The consultancy page should be clearer, more commercially oriented, and outcome-focused.

## Tone of design

- editorial but not flashy
- premium but not luxurious
- technical but not cold
- bold enough to feel memorable

## UX preferences

- Clear navigation across pages
- Strong visual hierarchy
- Good readability for long-form technical content
- Mobile experience should be first-class, not an afterthought
- CTA placement should feel deliberate and not overly aggressive

## Color direction

Current palette is dark blue and teal. This can evolve, but the redesign should:

- keep a professional and technical tone
- avoid generic purple-heavy SaaS aesthetics
- avoid flat or lifeless monochrome layouts
- use contrast carefully for readability and emphasis

## Typography direction

- Avoid default-looking system-only aesthetics unless strongly justified
- Prefer typography that feels modern, clean, and intentional
- Headings should carry personality and hierarchy
- Body text must remain highly readable, especially on portfolio content

## Layout direction

- Homepage should not be trapped in the current fixed-sidebar portfolio structure
- Portfolio can retain a more structured/documented feel
- Consultancy should read like a service page, not a resume
- Contact should be simple, direct, and uncluttered

## Motion direction

- Use subtle, meaningful motion
- Avoid excessive animation
- Motion should support hierarchy, navigation, and polish

## Content presentation rules

- Do not weaken the technical credibility of the portfolio
- Preserve the substance of existing portfolio content
- Prefer scannable layouts for experience and projects
- Keep service messaging practical and believable

## Anti-patterns to avoid

- generic startup landing page look
- overdesigned agency aesthetic
- too many visual gimmicks
- weak CTA hierarchy
- hiding navigation on mobile
- overly dense walls of text without structure
